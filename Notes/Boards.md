# Boards and the canvas

Shared boards add rules for anything that writes to `boards`: see `SharingBoards.md`.

- A board (`Board`, in `BoardStore`, persisted with Defaults) is a canvas in the Board tab: `statements` joined by `links` ("premise of"), and `sources`. It always holds at least one statement or source. Boards are English only.
- A `BoardSource` is a block of text or a web link on the canvas. It can't take arrows. Links only ever open in the external browser, never a view in the app. A new kind of content is a case in `BoardSource.Content` and in `SourceRecord.Content`.
- A statement's `source` puts it in that source's region. A region is never stored: `CanvasRegion` (ErgoLogic) works out its outline from where the source, cards and arrows are, hugging them so it goes in where there's a gap, and `CanvasRegionLayer` draws it. Dragging a source moves its cards too. A card dropped inside a region joins it, and one dropped far from its own leaves. Break Down makes one.
- A `Statement` is `id`, `position` (the box's centre) and `english`. `StatementEditorView` edits one statement of the current board, through `BoardStore`.
- New Board (`NewBoardMenu`, in the boards sheet and the wide sidebar) makes a blank board on a tap, and its menu offers templates (`BoardTemplate`, ErgoLogic: the cards and arrows, laid out by `CanvasLayout`). `BoardStore.newBoard(from:)` makes one as a draft: its cards are empty, with the template's prompt in `Statement.placeholder`, shown in the box and the editor until something's written. Every arrow in a template is honestly "premise of", so objections and rebuttals stand apart, unlinked. A template's own arrows don't stop a board being empty (`Board.isEmpty`), so one nobody writes on is discarded like a blank one.
- `BoardStore` is how statements and links change (add, update, move, delete, link, unlink, arrange). `Board.map` is the `ArgumentMap` that ErgoLogic's validator, layout and checks take.
- The canvas moves in UIKit (`CanvasBoardView`: pan, pinch, inertia, dragging, arrows), so nothing goes through SwiftUI while it moves. What a box looks like is SwiftUI (`StatementBoxView`), hosted per box, and the bars are SwiftUI (`CanvasBar`). Hosted boxes get no environment from the app: pass anything new in `CanvasBoard.boxContent`. Nothing on the canvas may be laid out from a measured size. On the Mac, the UIKit canvas files are `#if os(iOS)` and an AppKit `CanvasBoardView` (`Board View/AppKit/`, files suffixed `+AppKit`, with `CanvasBoard+AppKit.swift`) takes their place with the same API, so `CanvasBoard`'s callers and `CanvasBoardController` don't change. Every view in it is flipped, so its maths matches UIKit's, and the pan and zoom are `content`'s bounds rather than a transform, the way `NSScrollView` zooms. It adds hover handles, cursors, trackpad and wheel scrolling, right-click and keys, and has no haptics or drop lens. Links and text dragged in from other apps become sources where they're let go (`CanvasBoardView+Dropping+AppKit.swift`, read like shared items, through `CanvasBoard.dropSources` to `CanvasView.addSources(_:at:)`), never on a view-only board. On the Mac the canvas backdrop is the board's own background rather than the navigation container's, which there is the whole window.
- The statement editor opens out of its box with a UIKit transition (`StatementEditorPresenter`), since SwiftUI's zoom can't find a source view among separately hosted boxes. The editor is hosted too: pass anything new in `CanvasView.editorPresenter`. On the Mac, `StatementEditorPresenter` shows the same editor in a plain sheet.
- On a view-only board the canvas only pans and zooms, boxes and sources open to read. Hosted boxes learn this from `CanvasBoxModel.isReadOnly`, and `CanvasBoardView.isReadOnly` turns off dragging, connecting and the arrow menu.
- Undo is `CanvasHistory`, one step per change, through the window's `UndoManager`, and cleared when the board changes. `BoardTabView` owns it in the narrow layout, where the tab is never torn down. The wide layout drops the board's views when another section is chosen, so `WideLayout` owns it there and hands it down as `\.canvasHistory`, clearing it itself when the board changes off screen.
- The Library tab's search also finds boards (name, statements, sources), scratchpads and Ask chats (only when signed in here), archived ones included and marked (`PersonalSearch`, `Library Tab/Search/`). Stores are copied into `Sendable` snapshots on the main actor and matched off it: titles fuzzily, longer text as written, with a snippet around the match. A result opens through `NavigationManager.select`, as the sidebar does.
- The outline (`Board Tab/Outline/`, `CanvasView+Outline.swift`) is the same board as a list, chosen per device with `Defaults.Keys.boardLayout` (`BoardLayoutButton` in the toolbar). It takes the canvas's place inside `CanvasView`, so the bar, search, undo and view-only rules are the canvas's. Its structure is `ArgumentOutline` (ErgoLogic): final conclusions with premises nested, a shared premise expanded once, cycles still listed, then unlinked cards; sources and their cards come after. Rows only call `CanvasView`'s `edit`, `select`, `open`, `delete` and `deleteSource`, never `BoardStore` directly. While it's showing the canvas view is gone but `CanvasBoardController.view` still points at it, so the editor gets an empty controller (`editorBoxes`) and fades in; keep that, or it zooms from a box that isn't on screen.
- Export (`Board Tab/Export/`, in the board's ⋯ menu, viewers too) shares a file made only when it's chosen, in its own folder in the temporary directory: markdown from `ArgumentOutlineMarkdown` (ErgoLogic, same tree as the outline), or `BoardPicture` through `ImageRenderer` as a PDF or PNG. `BoardPicture` is its own SwiftUI `Canvas` drawing, never a snapshot of the canvas view (`ImageRenderer` can't draw UIKit or AppKit views, or the boxes' blur), and is scaled to at most `BoardPicture.maximumSide` points and the PNG to `BoardPictureFile.maximumPixels`, so a spread-out board can't allocate a huge image.
